# Journey 3 Reconciliation — Batch Is the Unit of Recommendation Applicability

Status: Adopted (production-facing)
Governed by: ArchitecturalReasoningStandard.md
Reconciles: Journey3.md's Section 5, O15 (evidence-first vs. trust-then-verify pacing question)

This document follows ArchitecturalReasoningStandard.md's bracketed claim-type notation (`[Observation]`, `[Inference]`, `[Decision]`) throughout. Resolution of O15 was explicitly deferred, in Journey3.md itself, to a future Journey 3 Reconciliation document; this is that document.

---

## Objective

[Decision] The governing constraint for this reconciliation, stated exactly once: determine whether, and on what basis, Companion may treat a batch's items as remaining actionable after time has passed within the same session, without requiring a new photo between individual item completions - resolving Journey3.md's O15 - while keeping that treatment inside the Evidence Boundary.

---

## Current Architecture (baseline)

Described without criticism - this is the production starting point this reconciliation confirms and now formally governs.

[Observation] `toggleBatchItem` (`App.js:1380-1387`) is synchronous, local-only React state with no evidence check: it flips a single item's status between `"checked"` and `"pending"` and logs an analytics event. Items may be toggled freely, in any order, any number of times.

[Observation] All items in a batch are generated by a single AI call in one response - one photo for the first batch, three photos (original, session-start, current) for later batches - not generated or evidenced individually. Direct code inspection confirmed no per-item independent generation, regeneration, or re-evidencing exists anywhere: toggling one item has no effect on any sibling item.

[Observation] `submitCompanionProgressPhoto` is the batch's own next natural evidence boundary: it requires a fresh photo, and its AI prompt (established by the 2026-07-18 "Companion batch workflow" decision) instructs: *"Trust photo 3 over what the user reported. The checklist reflects intent, not verified fact - if an item was marked done but photo 3 shows it clearly wasn't addressed, don't call out the discrepancy or tell the user they're wrong. Just generate the next batch naturally around what photo 3 actually shows."*

---

## Governing Decision

[Observation] No DecisionLog entry previously gated the per-item boundary specifically. O15 was the artifact's own explicitly-posed open question, marked by the wireframe itself as "an interaction design decision, not an architectural one," and confirmed NOT SETTLED by prior verification against the 2026-07-26 "Session Generation and Session Presentation" entry.

[Decision] DecisionLog.md's "2026-07-31 - Journey 3 - Batch Is the Unit of Recommendation Applicability" now governs this boundary directly. Quoted in full: "The batch, not the individual item, is Companion's unit of recommendation applicability. Once a batch is generated and presented, all its items remain actionable for the duration of the session without requiring a new photo between individual completions. Applicability is re-established, for the batch as a whole, at the next natural evidence boundary (the next progress photo)."

[Decision] DecisionLog.md's "2026-07-31 - Journey 3 - Mismatched Items Must Be Surfaced, Not Silently Reconciled (Supersedes Prior Silent-Reconciliation Instruction)" governs what happens when that trust turns out to be misplaced. Quoted in full: "This explicitly supersedes the 'don't call out the discrepancy' instruction in the batch-generation AI prompt (established by the 2026-07-18 'Companion batch workflow' decision). Going forward: if a fresh progress photo shows an item previously marked 'checked' was not actually addressed, Companion must resurface that item rather than silently generating around it. The resurfaced item must reach one of two explicit terminal states before it is treated as resolved: (a) the user confirms it's acceptable as-is (explicit dismissal), or (b) the user completes it. Until either occurs, the item continues to be resurfaced at future evidence checkpoints."

---

## Gap

[Observation] Prior to these two decisions, production behavior already permitted any batch item to be toggled at any time with no evidence check, and silently reconciled mismatches without surfacing them - but no adopted decision stated whether either was correct, and O15 remained an open question with no governing answer.

---

## Recommendation

[Decision] Adopt trust-then-verify at the batch grain, bounded by the batch's own next photo, as O15's resolution: items remain actionable for the duration of the session once a batch is generated, with no per-item evidence gate; applicability is re-checked, for the batch as a whole, only at the next progress photo. Any mismatch the fresh photo reveals - an item marked done but contradicted by the next photo, or an item never marked done but appearing resolved by different means - must be surfaced explicitly to the user, never silently reconciled or silently closed by Companion's own judgment, requiring explicit user dismissal or completion before being treated as resolved.

[Inference] This is what makes the decision honestly deliverable, not merely stated: the propose-then-user-confirms mechanism and the five-status Persistent Work model (Active / Carried / Completed / Accepted-as-is / Needs-Review (Resurfaced) - DecisionLog.md, "Persistent Work Item Identity and Matching"; ObjectModel.md, Persistent Work), both established later this same session, give "surface, don't silently reconcile" an actual mechanism to run on: a detected mismatch becomes a proposed match/Needs-Review item, put to the user rather than resolved by AI inference alone - the same discipline "Persistent Work Item Identity and Matching" requires generally. The original 2026-07-31 Journey 3 decision, quoted above, only specifies the checked-but-not-actually-done direction explicitly; the symmetric case (something that appears resolved without ever being marked done) is not separately decided in that entry's own text, but is handled the same way once the fuller Needs-Review status exists to receive it. This reconciliation notes that connection rather than asserting the original entry said more than it did.

---

## Consequences

**Changes:**
- The batch-generation AI prompt's "don't call out the discrepancy" instruction is superseded. A mismatched item must now be resurfaced instead of silently regenerated around.
- Session completion (the celebratory "you're done" moment) is withheld while any resurfaced item remains unresolved - navigation, pausing, and leaving the session remain unaffected and always available, matching every other flow in this product.
- Implementation must determine whether the existing `carried`/`skipped` item-status machinery in `currentBatch.items` already supports resurfacing, or needs extension - flagged for implementation, not decided here.

**Preserved:**
- `toggleBatchItem`'s synchronous, local, ungated behavior - no per-item evidence check is introduced.
- The single-AI-call, whole-batch generation model.
- `submitCompanionProgressPhoto` as the sole evidence boundary for the batch - no new reconciliation mechanism is introduced; the existing mechanism's *behavior on mismatch* changes, not its trigger or scope.
- Journey 4's "historical session context" language and Journey4Reconciliation.md require no amendment - Journey 4 already treated the resumed batch as one coherent unit, at the same grain this decision now formally adopts.

---

## Why This Is the Adopted Decision

[Decision] This was resolved as a direct product decision, not derived from the suspended constitutional investigation into whether AI-instructed item independence could itself satisfy the Evidence Boundary. That investigation's own interpretive limit remains unresolved and is not treated as settled by this reconciliation: it is genuinely possible for a single shared evidence source to support multiple items whose individual currency could diverge after generation, and nothing here proves otherwise in advance.

[Inference] What makes this decision compliant with the Evidence Boundary is not an advance proof that sibling items are independent - no such proof was attempted or achieved. It is that the system now commits to honestly detecting and surfacing the specific case where that assumption turns out to be wrong (a mismatched item, caught at the batch's own next photo), rather than silently absorbing the discrepancy the way the superseded prompt instruction did. The Evidence Boundary is satisfied through correction, not through advance proof of independence - trust is extended provisionally, and any point where it was misplaced is honestly surfaced rather than smoothed over.

[Decision] The suspended first-order/second-order inference question - whether item independence can be established architecturally rather than merely assumed and later checked - remains explicitly open. This reconciliation does not require it to be resolved, does not resolve it, and does not foreclose a future investigation that does. It is carried forward as still open, for whatever future question actually needs it answered.
